Have you ever watched a very young child learn something new?
They touch it. They lift it. They smell it. They put it in their mouth.
Children don't learn only with their eyes. They learn with their entire body.
But something happened when the screen arrived. A hundred images per second. A thousand colours. A thousand sounds. And somewhere behind all that visual overflow — a deep sensory hunger.
A child who sees everything — and touches nothing.
My name is Dr. Adel F. Amer. And this is the Juthur Project.
Juthur — in Arabic — means Roots.
And the Juthur Project is built on one simple, profound idea: What your hands touch — your mind remembers.
In 2024, I developed a theory I call the Tactile Reading Framework — TRF.
The theory says one thing with complete clarity: The Arabic letter cannot be learned through sight alone. It must be felt. Weighed. Warmed. Heard. It must live in the body before it can live in the mind.
Juthur was born from a single moment I will never forget.
I was sitting with a seven-year-old girl. She held the Arabic letter Baa between her fingers. She drew it in the air. Then she looked at me and said: I know it now. I felt it.
That moment was the beginning of Juthur.
Today, the Juthur Project is: First, a scientific theory with six core principles — Synchrony, Gradation, Specification, Window, Wholeness, Integration.
Second, four academic books — The Theory, The Field Guide, The Creative Library, and Research and Assessment.
Third, twenty literary-pedagogical journeys — from the first breath to the final harvest.
Fourth, validated, documented instruments — the Lathatha Index, the Bawsala, the Mirsah, Jumbaz al-Huruf, and the Anfas Ritual.
In this video series, you will discover: How the theory works — and what it says to modern neuroscience. How Juthur stories are read — with voice, movement, and rhythm. How to apply the project in your home, your school, anywhere a child is learning.
Juthur is not a remedial programme for struggling readers. Juthur is for every child. Because every child deserves to read with their whole body — not only with their eyes.
The body does not read the letter. The body reads the relationship between the letter and the space, between the weight and the effort, between the cold and the time.
Juthur Reads. The first root has been planted.
