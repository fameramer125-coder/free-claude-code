In 2020, the world went quiet.
Schools closed. Children stayed home. And something happened that we are only beginning to understand.
The screen replaced the classroom. The pixel replaced the teacher's hand on the shoulder. The chat replaced the whisper between friends.
Absent in the Time of the Pandemic — Ghaaib fi Zaman al-Jaaihah — is one of the central texts of the Juthur Project.
It is a study. A narrative. And a warning.
The title carries a deliberate ambiguity. Who is absent? The child, removed from school? Or the body — removed from learning?
The pandemic did not create the problem. It revealed it.
The problem was already there — the slow disappearance of the body from education. The screen accelerated what was already underway.
This text argues that the body's absence from learning is not a logistical problem. It is a cognitive emergency.
When the body is absent from the act of reading — reading becomes decoding. And decoding is not reading.
Reading is meaning. Meaning lives in the body. Restore the body — and you restore the reading.
This is the lesson of the pandemic. And this is the foundation of Juthur.
